        How the fishability score works
        • Water temperature (32%) against the chosen species' comfort range. Trout score zero-ish at 68°F and up, because fighting fish in warm water can kill them.
        • Light (20%): trout favor low light and overcast; smallmouth warm up in the afternoon in cool months.
        • Wind (16%): casting gets hard above 15 mph.
        • Rain and storms (12%), barometer (10%) and solunar timing (10%).
        • The day is then scaled by river flow (stable or dropping after a bump is best; a spike means muddy water) and season (run timing for steelhead and salmon).
        • Water temperature and flow come from the nearest matching USGS stream gauge. Without one, water temperature is estimated from recent air temperature and the river type, and marked as an estimate.

        Weather: Open-Meteo. Flows: USGS Water Data. River coordinates are approximate access points. Always check state regulations before fishing.

        If flow data stops loading, USGS may be rate limiting. A free key from api.waterdata.usgs.gov/signup lifts the limit.
